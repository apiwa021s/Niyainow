## What Big Novel Platforms Converge On

1. Discovery is not a single grid. Production platforms split entry points into ranked lists, latest updates, new releases, completed stories, genre funnels, and personalized/resume rails.
2. The homepage works best as a command center. A reader should be able to continue reading, search, open a hot ranking, browse by genre, or jump to latest chapters within the first viewport.
3. Latest chapters should read as a live feed. A grid of covers is attractive, but a timestamped chapter list is faster for repeat readers who visit daily.
4. Reader comfort is a product feature, not a setting afterthought. Theme, font size, width, night/sepia/AMOLED modes, and resume position are expected.
5. Rankings need clear time semantics. "Popular this week", "rising", "completed", and "latest updates" solve different reader intents.
6. Mobile IA should be thumb-first. Search, browse, library, profile, and continue-reading need shorter paths than desktop menus.
7. The UI should make content status visible: ongoing/completed/hiatus, free/locked, chapter count, rating, and latest update recency.

## Product Direction For This Repo

NiyaiThai should keep its current dense editorial base, but reshape the public experience around four production lanes:

- Read now: continue-reading and the main featured story.
- Discover: genre chips, search, recommendations, new this week.
- Follow the pulse: latest chapter feed and weekly rankings.
- Trust the catalog: completed stories, status labels, ratings, chapter counts, and clear filters.

The reader mode is already close to production expectations. The main gap is the public homepage and live-update presentation.
